\section*{1. Union-Find Data Structure}

\vspace{-10pt}
\subsection*{(a)}

Parent array after each operation ($\mathrm{parent}[i] = i$ means $i$ is a root):
\[
\begin{array}{l|ccccccc}
\text{Operation} & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 \\ \hline
\text{Init} & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 \\
\textsc{Union}(1,3) & 1 & 2 & 1 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 \\
\textsc{Union}(1,7) & 1 & 2 & 1 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 1 \\
\textsc{Union}(2,5) & 1 & 2 & 1 & 4 & 2 & 6 & 1 \\
\textsc{Union}(4,6) & 1 & 2 & 1 & 4 & 2 & 4 & 1 \\
\textsc{Union}(2,3) & 1 & 1 & 1 & 4 & 2 & 4 & 1
\end{array}
\]

\textsc{Union}(1,3): $|T_1|=|T_3|=1$ (tie), $3 \to 1$. \quad
\textsc{Union}(1,7): $|T_7|=1 < 2=|T_1|$, $7 \to 1$.

\textsc{Union}(2,5): $|T_2|=|T_5|=1$ (tie), $5 \to 2$. \quad
\textsc{Union}(4,6): $|T_4|=|T_6|=1$ (tie), $6 \to 4$.

\textsc{Union}(2,3): $\textsc{Find}(2)=2$, $\textsc{Find}(3)=1$. $|T_2|=2 < 3=|T_1|$, $2 \to 1$.

$\textsc{Find}(5)$: $5 \to 2 \to 1$.
\[\boxed{\textsc{Find}(5) = 1}\]

\vspace{-20pt}
\subsection*{(b)}

\textbf{Claim:} A union-by-size tree of height $t$ has $\ge 2^t$ vertices.

\textbf{Base ($t = 0$):} Height $0$ $\implies$ single vertex. $|T| = 1 \ge 2^0$. \checkmark

\textbf{Inductive step:} Assume for all $t' < t$. Let $T$ be a tree whose height first reaches $t$ via $\textsc{Union}(T_s, T_l)$ with $|T_s| \le |T_l|$ (root of $T_s$ attached to root of $T_l$).

For the height to increase: $\text{height}(T_s) + 1 = t$, so $\text{height}(T_s) = t - 1$.
\begin{align*}
&\text{By Inductive Hypothesis:} \quad |T_s| \ge 2^{t-1} \\
&|T_l| \ge |T_s| \implies |T_l| \ge 2^{t-1} \\
&|T| = |T_s| + |T_l| \ge 2^{t-1} + 2^{t-1} = 2^t \qquad \qed
\end{align*}
\[\boxed{\text{height}(T) \le \lfloor \log_2 |T| \rfloor}\]

\vspace{-20pt}
\subsection*{(c)}

Without union-by-size, the direction of each union is arbitrary. We build a chain:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l|l}
\text{Step} & \text{Operation} & \text{Direction chosen} \\ \hline
1 & \textsc{Union}(1,2) & 1 \to 2 \\
2 & \textsc{Union}(3,2) & 2 \to 3 \\
3 & \textsc{Union}(4,3) & 3 \to 4 \\
4 & \textsc{Union}(5,4) & 4 \to 5 \\
5 & \textsc{Union}(6,5) & 5 \to 6 \\
6 & \textsc{Union}(7,6) & 7 \to 6
\end{array}
\]

Final tree (root $= 6$): path $6 \to 5 \to 4 \to 3 \to 2 \to 1$, with $7$ as child of $6$.
\[\boxed{\text{height} = 5}\]

\subsection*{(c)}

Directed graph with negative edge weight where Dijkstra fails.

$V = \{1, \ldots, 6\}$, $s = 1$. Edges:

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
\text{edge} & 1 \to 2 & 1 \to 3 & 3 \to 2 & 2 \to 4 & 3 \to 5 & 4 \to 6 & 5 \to 6 \\ \hline
w & 1 & 5 & -10 & 3 & 2 & 1 & 4
\end{array}
\]

\textbf{Dijkstra} settles vertex $2$ at $d[2] = 1$ (iter 2). When vertex $3$ is later settled (iter 4), the edge $3 \to 2$ with $w = -10$ gives $d[3] + w = 5 + (-10) = -5 < 1 = d[2]$, but vertex $2$ is already finalized.

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccc}
v & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 \\ \hline
\text{Dijkstra } d[v] & 0 & 1 & 5 & 4 & 7 & 5 \\
\text{Correct } d^*[v] & 0 & -5 & 5 & -2 & 7 & -1
\end{array}
\]

Correct path to $2$: $1 \to 3 \to 2$, weight $5 + (-10) = -5$. Dijkstra returns $d[2] = 1 \ne -5$.
\[\boxed{\text{Dijkstra fails on vertices } 2, 4, 6}\]

\section*{3. Bellman-Ford Algorithm}

\vspace{-10pt}
\subsection*{(a)}

Suppose $G$ contains a negative cycle $C = v_1 \to v_2 \to \cdots \to v_k \to v_1$ reachable from $s$.

Since $C$ is reachable, after $n - 1$ rounds of Bellman-Ford, $d(v_i) < \infty$ for each $v_i \in C$.

Suppose for contradiction $d(u) + w(u,v) \ge d(v)$ for every edge $(u,v) \in E$. Sum over all edges of $C$:
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{k} \bigl[d(v_i) + w(v_i, v_{i+1})\bigr] \ge \sum_{i=1}^{k} d(v_{i+1})
\]
Since $v_{k+1} = v_1$, the multisets $\{v_1, \ldots, v_k\}$ and $\{v_2, \ldots, v_{k+1}\}$ are identical. The $d$-value sums cancel:
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{k} w(v_i, v_{i+1}) \ge 0
\]
This contradicts $C$ being a negative cycle. \qed

\vspace{-20pt}
\subsection*{(b)}

$V = \{1, \ldots, 6\}$, $s = 1$, 10 directed edges:

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccccccc}
\text{edge} & 1{\to}2 & 2{\to}3 & 3{\to}4 & 4{\to}5 & 5{\to}6 & 1{\to}3 & 1{\to}4 & 2{\to}4 & 3{\to}5 & 2{\to}6 \\ \hline
w & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & -5 & 10 & 10 & 10 & 10 & 10
\end{array}
\]

Shortest path to vertex $6$: $1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 4 \to 5 \to 6$, weight $1{+}1{+}1{+}1{+}({-}5) = -1$, using $5 = n - 1$ edges.

\textbf{Trace} with worst-case edge ordering ($5{\to}6, 4{\to}5, 3{\to}4, 2{\to}3, 1{\to}2$, then remaining):

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccc}
\text{Round} & d[1] & d[2] & d[3] & d[4] & d[5] & d[6] \\ \hline
0 & 0 & \infty & \infty & \infty & \infty & \infty \\
1 & 0 & 1 & 10 & 10 & 20 & 11 \\
2 & 0 & 1 & 2 & 10 & 11 & 11 \\
3 & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 11 & 6 \\
4 & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 6 \\
5 & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & -1
\end{array}
\]

After 4 rounds: $d[6] = 6$. After 5 rounds: $d[6] = -1$.
\[\boxed{d[6] = 6 \ne -1 = d^*(6) \text{ after 4 rounds}}\]
